Models of the dielectric function

The parametrised forms difes offers, what each describes, the meaning and units of its parameters, its passivity condition, and its name in FOCUS.

Every model is

\varepsilon(\omega) = \varepsilon_\infty \prod_j F_j(\omega) + \sum_k \chi_k(\omega),

with additive terms \chi_k (Drude, Lorentz, causal Gaussian, user formulas) and multiplicative factors F_j (four-parameter modes). A model may mix both, as FOCUS does when a four-parameter product for the lattice is completed by additive Gaussian bands; then \varepsilon_\infty multiplies the product only (DielectricModel.epsilon).

Drude

Free carriers of plasma frequency \omega_p and scattering rate \gamma (Drude(wp, gamma)):

\chi_D(\omega) = -\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2 + i\gamma\omega}, \qquad \chi_D'' = \frac{\omega_p^2\gamma}{\omega(\omega^2 + \gamma^2)} \ge 0 .

\omega_p^2 = N e^2/(\varepsilon_0 m^*) in SI, converted to \mathrm{cm}^{-1}; 1/\gamma is the momentum relaxation time. The dc conductivity is \sigma_0 = 2\pi\varepsilon_0 c\,\omega_p^2/\gamma (with \omega_p and \gamma in \mathrm{cm}^{-1}). Passive for \omega_p, \gamma \ge 0. FOCUS: drude(x, wp, g). Two Drude terms (a two-carrier or a “Drude plus broad background” description, as in the TFG report’s Table 4 for chromium) are strongly correlated in a fit over a limited range; the fitter’s errors say so.

Lorentz oscillator

A damped harmonic oscillator at \omega_0 with damping \gamma and plasma frequency \omega_p (Lorentz(w0, wp, gamma)):

\chi_L(\omega) = \frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 - i\gamma\omega}, \qquad \chi_L'' = \frac{\omega_p^2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2} \ge 0 .

The strength S = \Delta\varepsilon = \omega_p^2/\omega_0^2 is the oscillator’s contribution to \varepsilon(0); FOCUS and the TFG report parametrise by it, phonon-3p(x, dEps, w0, g), and Lorentz.from_strength(w0, S, gamma) converts. Passive for \omega_p, \gamma \ge 0. The Drude term is the Lorentz term with \omega_0 = 0. Each polar phonon of a crystal is one such term in the classical (Born–Huang) dispersion theory; its damping is frequency-independent, which is the model’s main limitation for strong modes.

Four-parameter semi-quantum model

The factorised form (Berreman & Unterwald 1968; Gervais & Piriou 1974), one factor per polar mode with independent TO and LO frequencies and dampings (FourParameterMode(w_to, g_to, w_lo, g_lo)):

\varepsilon(\omega) = \varepsilon_\infty \prod_j \frac{\omega_{LO,j}^2 - \omega^2 - i\gamma_{LO,j}\,\omega}{\omega_{TO,j}^2 - \omega^2 - i\gamma_{TO,j}\,\omega} .

The zeros of \varepsilon are at the LO modes, its poles at the TO modes, and the LO damping is a parameter of its own, which the Lorentz sum cannot provide (there the LO damping is fixed by the TO ones). With \gamma_{LO} = \gamma_{TO} one factor reduces to 1 + (\omega_{LO}^2 - \omega_{TO}^2)/(\omega_{TO}^2 - \omega^2 - i\gamma\omega), a Lorentz oscillator of strength \varepsilon_\infty(\omega_{LO}^2 - \omega_{TO}^2)/\omega_{TO}^2. The static constant follows the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller relation. FOCUS: phonon-4p(x, wTO, gTO, wLO, gLO), its principal model for crystals; the quartz-E and \mathrm{LiNbO_{3}}-E examples of its help are of this form.

Passivity is not automatic. The imaginary part of one factor is proportional to \omega[\gamma_{TO}(\omega_{LO}^2 - \omega^2) - \gamma_{LO}(\omega_{TO}^2 - \omega^2)], which is negative below \omega_{TO} when \gamma_{LO} > \gamma_{TO} and above \omega_{LO} when \gamma_{LO} < \gamma_{TO}. The product of all modes is passive at high frequency if \sum_j(\gamma_{LO,j} - \gamma_{TO,j}) \ge 0, and between modes only if the neighbouring modes compensate; small negative \varepsilon'' between modes (of order 10^{-2} in FOCUS’s quartz example) are a known feature of the model and harmless for R. As implemented: the fitter adds the penalty \lambda\max(0, \sum_j(\gamma_{TO,j} - \gamma_{LO,j})) with \lambda = 10^3 by default (fit(passivity_penalty=)), and the refractive index takes the principal root so that a slightly negative \varepsilon'' gives a slightly negative k rather than a negative n.

The semi-quantum interpretation (De Sousa Meneses et al. 2004) generalises the constant dampings to frequency-dependent self-energies, FOCUS’s phonon-anharmonic and phonon-anharmonic-i; those profiles are not in difes yet.

Causal Gaussian band

Disordered solids (glasses, amorphous films) show bands that are Gaussian rather than Lorentzian, because a distribution of local environments spreads the mode frequency. A Gaussian \chi'' alone is not causal; the causal form (De Sousa Meneses et al. 2004; FOCUS’s gaussian-kkg) is the Hilbert-transform pair (CausalGaussian(w0, A, sigma)):

\chi''(\omega) = A\left[e^{-a^2(\omega - \omega_0)^2} - e^{-a^2(\omega + \omega_0)^2}\right], \qquad \chi'(\omega) = \frac{2A}{\sqrt\pi}\left[D\big(a(\omega + \omega_0)\big) - D\big(a(\omega - \omega_0)\big)\right],

with a = 2\sqrt{\ln 2}/\sigma so that \sigma is the full width at half maximum of the band, and D(x) = e^{-x^2}\int_0^x e^{t^2}dt the Dawson function. The second exponential and the first Dawson term are the mirror band at -\omega_0 that makes \chi'' odd; they matter only for bands close to zero frequency. A is the peak value of \chi'' (dimensionless). Passive for A \ge 0. As implemented: the normalisation is the one above; the 2004 paper’s convention for the amplitude is to be confirmed against the paper (the roadmap lists it), which affects only the meaning of A, not the shape.

Causal Voigt band

FOCUS’s voigt-kkg (difes voigt_kkg(x, A, x_0, w_G, w_L)) is the causal version of the Voigt profile, a Gaussian of FWHM w_G convolved with a Lorentzian of FWHM w_L. Both parts come from one function, the Faddeeva function w(z) = e^{-z^2}\,\mathrm{erfc}(-iz): its real part is the Voigt profile and its imaginary part the Hilbert transform of it, so with X_\pm = 2\sqrt{\ln 2}\,(\omega \pm \omega_0)/w_G and Y = \sqrt{\ln 2}\,w_L/w_G,

\chi''(\omega) = \frac{A}{N}\left[\operatorname{Re} w(X_- + iY) - \operatorname{Re} w(X_+ + iY)\right], \qquad \chi'(\omega) = \frac{A}{N}\left[\operatorname{Im} w(X_+ + iY) - \operatorname{Im} w(X_- + iY)\right].

The mirror band at -\omega_0 makes \chi'' odd, as causality requires. For w_L \to 0 the Faddeeva function reduces to the Gaussian and the Dawson function, and the band becomes the causal Gaussian above. As implemented: the normalisation N is FOCUS’s, found against FOCUS’s own exported dielectric function of its Borofloat sample project: N = \operatorname{Re} w(iY) - \operatorname{Re} w(2X_0 + iY), the value of the mirrored pair at the band centre, so that \chi''(\omega_0) = A exactly. For a band narrower than its centre N = 1 to all digits that matter; for a band wider than its centre (Borofloat’s 81 \mathrm{cm}^{-1} band with w_G = 351) it is 2.2, and without it the band is wrong by that factor. The causal Gaussian keeps the published form (De Sousa Meneses et al. 2004, and eq. 4 of the fluoride paper) without this renormalisation: a one-band FOCUS export of a Gaussian four times wider than its centre (4 October 2026) agrees with the published form to 10^{-6} and rules the renormalised pair out for the Gaussian. For the Voigt, three one-band exports at increasing w_L show FOCUS’s band has the exact construction’s peak, width and area, with an oscillatory residual of 1.5\times10^{-3} of the peak in the Gaussian limit and 2\times10^{-4} at w_L = 2w_G: FOCUS’s own approximation of the Voigt function, reproduced by none of the standard algorithms. difes evaluates the exact function.

Fano-shaped Lorentz oscillator

RefFIT’s codes (−65, −66), after Damascelli (1996); in difes FanoLorentz(w0, wp, gamma, wq):

\chi(\omega) = \frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 - i\gamma\omega}\left(1 + i\,\frac{\omega_q}{\omega}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\omega_p\,\omega_q}{\omega_0\,\omega}\right)^2 .

An asymmetric line that is Kramers–Kronig consistent and sum-rule friendly, which many Fano-like forms are not; \omega_q = 0 gives the symmetric Lorentzian, and the conventional asymmetry parameter is q = \omega_0/\omega_q. Its \chi'' can be negative on one side of the line, so the term needs a positive background to sit on, as RefFIT’s manual warns. Analytic derivatives for the fitter; test_reffit checks the reduction to the Lorentzian, the causality and the derivatives.

Tauc–Lorentz oscillator

Jellison and Modine (1996, with the erratum), RefFIT’s codes (−55, −56); in difes TaucLorentz(w0, wg, A, gamma):

\chi''(\omega) = \frac{A\,\omega_0\,\gamma\,(\omega - \omega_g)^2}{\omega\left[(\omega^2 - \omega_0^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2\right]}\ \Theta(\omega - \omega_g),

a Lorentzian multiplied by the Tauc joint density of states above the gap \omega_g, and \chi' its Kramers–Kronig transform in the closed form of the paper. The two logarithmic terms that diverge at \omega = \omega_g are combined, their coefficient being -(\omega-\omega_g)^2, so the gap point is regular. The term is the susceptibility: RefFIT lists \varepsilon_1 = 1 + \dots, and the 1 belongs to \varepsilon_\infty here. It needs 4\omega_0^2 > \gamma^2. test_reffit compares the closed-form \chi' with a numerical transform of \chi'' on a grid to 200 000 \mathrm{cm}^{-1}: they agree to 10^{-7} of the scale.

User formulas

ExpressionTerm("name(x, a, b, ...) = expression", a=..., b=...) makes any formula of the math language an additive term; its arguments after the variable become parameters with bounds and frozen flags, fitted like any other. Causality and passivity are the author’s responsibility: check with kk.causality_error and by plotting \chi''. Typical uses: a profile FOCUS has and difes does not (imported from OSML), a temperature-dependent parametrisation, a background.

Parameters at a glance

term parameters unit passive when FOCUS
Drude \(\omega_p\), \(\gamma\) \(\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\) both \(\ge 0\) drude(x, wp, g)
Lorentz \(\omega_0\), \(\omega_p\), \(\gamma\); \(S = \omega_p^2/\omega_0^2\) \(\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\); \(S\) dimensionless \(\omega_p, \gamma \ge 0\) phonon-3p(x, dEps, w0, g)
four-parameter mode \(\omega_{TO}\), \(\gamma_{TO}\), \(\omega_{LO}\), \(\gamma_{LO}\) \(\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\) all modes together, \(\sum(\gamma_{LO} - \gamma_{TO}) \ge 0\) phonon-4p(x, wTO, gTO, wLO, gLO)
causal Gaussian \(\omega_0\), \(A\), \(\sigma\) (FWHM) \(\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\); \(A\) dimensionless \(A \ge 0\) gaussian-kkg (normalisation to confirm)
\(\varepsilon_\infty\) dimensionless \(\ge 1\) physically; bound \(\ge 0\) the leading number of the product

Analytic derivatives of \chi with respect to every parameter are implemented for Drude, Lorentz and Gaussian (Term.d_epsilon) and used by the fitter’s Jacobian; the four-parameter mode and formulas use finite differences.

    What this is

    The test ledger of difes, a program for dielectric-function models, reflectivity, emissivity, Kramers–Kronig and fitting of infrared spectra, built as a personal project to give the group an open and documented counterpart of FOCUS. Every check the program has passed through is listed here with its grade, its data and its result, next to the validation pages the checks come from and the complete manual: how the app looks and works, and the physics it implements, equation by equation.
