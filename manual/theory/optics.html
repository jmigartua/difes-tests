From \varepsilon to the measured spectrum

Fresnel’s coefficients at normal incidence; reflectivity and emissivity of an opaque sample; transmission, reflectivity and emissivity of a slab, coherent and incoherent; a film on an opaque substrate.

The interface

A plane wave from vacuum meets a medium of index \tilde n at normal incidence. Continuity of the tangential fields gives the amplitude coefficients

\tilde r = \frac{\tilde n - 1}{\tilde n + 1}, \qquad \tilde t = \frac{2}{\tilde n + 1},

and the reflected fraction of power R = |\tilde r|^2 (optics.amplitude_reflection, reflectivity_normal):

R = \frac{(n - 1)^2 + k^2}{(n + 1)^2 + k^2} .

The phase \theta = \arg\tilde r is zero for a transparent medium with n > 1 and tends to \pi for a metal (k \gg n). Inverting, \tilde n = (1 + \tilde r)/(1 - \tilde r) (r_theta_to_nk): this is why R and \theta together give \varepsilon.

At oblique incidence the coefficients split into s and p polarisations; FOCUS’s Fresnel library has them and difes reads that library, but the built-in optics are normal incidence. The group’s emissivity measurements at 10° differ from normal incidence by less than 10^{-3} in R for the materials in question, below the transcription precision of the parameters.

Opaque sample

A sample thick enough that nothing returns from its back face (a metal, a crystal in its reststrahlen band, any absorbing solid with \alpha d \gg 1):

R = |\tilde r|^2, \qquad E = 1 - R .

The second relation is Kirchhoff’s law: at thermal equilibrium the spectral directional emissivity equals the absorptivity, and with no transmission the absorptivity is 1 - R (emissivity_opaque). It holds for each direction and polarisation separately, so a normal emissivity compares with a normal reflectivity.

Free-standing slab

A slab of thickness d in vacuum, with r_1 = (1 - \tilde n)/(1 + \tilde n) the coefficient of the vacuum-to-medium interface and \beta = 2\pi\tilde n\omega d the complex single-pass phase.

Coherent (the slab is plane-parallel and thinner than the coherence length, so the multiply reflected waves interfere; fringes appear). Summing the geometric series of internal reflections, the Airy formulas (_slab_amplitudes):

\tilde r_{slab} = \frac{r_1\left(1 - e^{2i\beta}\right)}{1 - r_1^2 e^{2i\beta}}, \qquad \tilde t_{slab} = \frac{\left(1 - r_1^2\right)e^{i\beta}}{1 - r_1^2 e^{2i\beta}}, \qquad R = |\tilde r_{slab}|^2,\quad T = |\tilde t_{slab}|^2 .

Incoherent (a wedged or rough slab, or a resolution coarser than the fringe spacing): the intensities of the internal reflections add, not the amplitudes. With R_1 = |r_1|^2 the single-interface reflectivity and a = e^{-\alpha d} = e^{-4\pi k\omega d} the single-pass intensity attenuation,

T = \frac{(1 - R_1)^2\,a}{1 - R_1^2 a^2}, \qquad R = R_1 + \frac{(1 - R_1)^2 R_1\,a^2}{1 - R_1^2 a^2} .

In both cases the emissivity of the slab, by Kirchhoff’s law with transmission, is

E = 1 - R - T

(transmission_slab, reflectivity_slab, emissivity_slab). FOCUS’s Reflectance-E, Transmittance-E and Emittance-E library symbols are these same formulas (the incoherent ones; Reflectance-E-IE the coherent one), and importing those libraries into difes and evaluating them reproduces the functions above to machine precision, which is a test of the package.

Limits worth knowing: for a \to 0 (opaque) T \to 0 and R \to R_1; for k = 0 and coherent, R + T = 1 exactly.

Film on an opaque substrate

A film of index \tilde n and thickness d on a substrate of index \tilde n_s thick enough to be opaque (the oxidised chromium: an oxide film on the metal). With r_1 = (1 - \tilde n)/(1 + \tilde n) at the vacuum–film interface, r_2 = (\tilde n - \tilde n_s)/(\tilde n + \tilde n_s) at the film–substrate interface and \beta as before:

coherent (reflectivity_film):

\tilde r = \frac{r_1 + r_2\,e^{2i\beta}}{1 + r_1 r_2\,e^{2i\beta}}, \qquad R = |\tilde r|^2 ;

incoherent, with R_1 = |r_1|^2, R_2 = |r_2|^2 and A = |e^{2i\beta}|^2 = e^{-2\alpha d}:

R = R_1 + \frac{(1 - R_1)^2 R_2\,A}{1 - R_1 R_2\,A} ;

and in both cases E = 1 - R, there being no transmission (emissivity_film). A substrate with \tilde n_s = 1 turns the film formulas into the slab ones, which is a test.

As implemented. Two incoherent forms are offered. The default, form="energy", is the energy-conserving one above, with interface transmittance 1 - R_1 both ways. FOCUS’s Reflectance-Ei-E1-E2 symbol and eq. 27 of the TFG report write the numerator as |(1 - r_1^2)\, r_2\, \tau^2|^2, the product of the amplitude transmittances; this is form="focus". The two coincide when the film’s index is real and differ where the film absorbs: for Irati’s \mathrm{Cr_{2}O_{3}} film they differ by up to 0.05 in the reststrahlen band (Validation page, level A). An earlier version of this paragraph claimed agreement to better than 1 %; that held for the weakly absorbing case tested then and is withdrawn in general. Choose focus to reproduce a FOCUS result exactly; the energy form is the one consistent with the slab formulas. When d \to 0 the incoherent film does not reduce to the bare substrate (the intensity sum has no phase to cancel), whereas the coherent one does; for very thin films use the coherent form.

Effective-medium layer (as FOCUS used it)

Irati’s FOCUS projects (log L039) put a second film over the oxide: a layer of air and oxide mixed at a volume fraction f of inclusions, with the Bruggeman dielectric function. For two components \varepsilon_m (host) and \varepsilon_i (inclusions) the Bruggeman condition \sum_j f_j (\varepsilon_j - \varepsilon)/(\varepsilon_j + 2\varepsilon) = 0 is a quadratic in \varepsilon:

\varepsilon_{\rm eff} = \frac{b \pm \sqrt{b^2 + 8\,\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_m}}{4}, \qquad b = 3f(\varepsilon_i - \varepsilon_m) - \varepsilon_i + 2\varepsilon_m,

and the physical root is the one with \operatorname{Im}\varepsilon_{\rm eff} \ge 0 (FOCUS’s Eeff-BG symbol chooses it with an if-else on the sign). difes evaluates the imported symbol as a formula; the layer then enters the nested incoherent-film formula of the project as the top film. An effective-medium sample geometry of the core, with f and the thickness as fit parameters, is on the roadmap.

Which geometry, which quantity

geometry options R T E
opaque bulk none \(|\tilde r|^2\) — \(1 - R\)
slab, coherent thickness Airy Airy \(1 - R - T\)
slab, incoherent thickness, coherent=False intensity sum intensity sum \(1 - R - T\)
film on opaque substrate thickness, substrate coherent or incoherent — \(1 - R\)

Derivatives for the fitter

For a bulk R (and E = 1 - R) the fitter uses the chain rule analytically: with \tilde r = (\tilde n - 1)/(\tilde n + 1), d\tilde r/d\tilde n = 2/(\tilde n + 1)^2 and d\tilde n/d\varepsilon = 1/(2\tilde n),

dR = 2\,\mathrm{Re}\left(\tilde r^*\,d\tilde r\right) = \mathrm{Re}\!\left[\frac{2\,\tilde r^*}{\tilde n\,(\tilde n + 1)^2}\;d\varepsilon\right],

and d\varepsilon/dp from each term’s d_epsilon. Slabs and films use finite differences.

    What this is

    The test ledger of difes, a program for dielectric-function models, reflectivity, emissivity, Kramers–Kronig and fitting of infrared spectra, built as a personal project to give the group an open and documented counterpart of FOCUS. Every check the program has passed through is listed here with its grade, its data and its result, next to the validation pages the checks come from and the complete manual: how the app looks and works, and the physics it implements, equation by equation.
