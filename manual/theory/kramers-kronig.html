Kramers–Kronig on a finite range

The transforms, their closed-form evaluation on a piecewise polynomial, the error of a finite range, and the corrections: multiply subtractive relations and the constrained finite-range correction of De Sousa Meneses et al. 2007.

The two transforms

From the Response page, for a spectrum known on [0, \infty):

\chi'(\omega) = \frac{2}{\pi}\,P\!\!\int_0^\infty \frac{s\,\chi''(s)}{s^2 - \omega^2}\,ds \qquad\text{(2007, Eq. 5)}

\theta(\omega) = -\frac{2\omega}{\pi}\,P\!\!\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln r(s)}{s^2 - \omega^2}\,ds = -\frac{1}{\pi}\,P\!\!\int_0^\infty \ln\left|\frac{s + \omega}{s - \omega}\right| \frac{d\ln r(s)}{ds}\,ds \qquad\text{(2007, Eq. 1)}

with r = \sqrt R and \theta the phase of \tilde r = (\tilde n - 1)/(\tilde n + 1). The second form of the phase integral follows from the first by parts; on an infinite range the boundary term vanishes because \ln|(s + \omega)/(s - \omega)| \to 0 at both ends.

A measurement gives the spectrum on [a, b] only. Replacing the limits by a and b defines the truncated transforms \chi'^{ab}_{KK} and \theta^{ab}_{KK} (2007, Eq. 7 and 15), which differ from the true functions by the contribution of [0, a) and (b, \infty):

\Delta\chi'^{ab}(\omega) = \chi'(\omega) - \chi'^{ab}_{KK}(\omega) = \frac{2}{\pi}\left[P\!\!\int_0^a + P\!\!\int_b^\infty\right]\frac{s\,\chi''(s)}{s^2 - \omega^2}\,ds .

The key observation of the 2007 paper is that this error is a smooth function of \omega inside [a, b], fixed mainly by the weight and position of the missing spectral features and only weakly by their shape; so it can be represented by a few parameters and determined from little extra information.

As implemented. On a finite range the two forms of the phase integral are not equal: integrating by parts on [a, b] leaves

\theta^{ab}_{deriv}(\omega) = \theta^{ab}_{direct}(\omega) - \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\ln r(b)\ln\left|\frac{b + \omega}{b - \omega}\right| - \ln r(a)\ln\left|\frac{a + \omega}{a - \omega}\right|\right].

The derivative form (Eq. 15) is the default of kk.phase_kk; it is the better estimate on a truncated range because the boundary term it removes is exactly the spurious contribution of the two artificial edges where \ln r is cut. On a synthetic Lorentz spectrum truncated to 250–2500 \mathrm{cm}^{-1} the derivative form has a mean phase error of 0.016 where the direct form has 0.6. The direct form (form="direct") is kept for comparison with OptiCFRC, which used it.

Evaluating the principal value in closed form

The integrand is known at the measured points s_0 < s_1 < \dots < s_N. Interpolate it by a piecewise polynomial P_i(s) of degree n on each interval [s_i, s_{i+1}] (2007, Eq. 2–3; n = 3, a natural cubic spline, in difes). On each interval, for a point c inside or outside it,

P\!\!\int_{s_i}^{s_{i+1}} \frac{P_i(s)}{s - c}\,ds = P_i(c)\Big[\ln|s - c|\Big]_{s_i}^{s_{i+1}} + \int_{s_i}^{s_{i+1}} \frac{P_i(s) - P_i(c)}{s - c}\,ds ,

and the second integral is an ordinary polynomial integral, because (P_i(s) - P_i(c))/(s - c) divides exactly. Summing over intervals and regrouping the logarithms by node, the coefficient of \ln|s_i - c| is P_{i-1}(c) - P_i(c), which is zero when c = s_i by continuity of the interpolant; so an interior node hit by the output point contributes no singular term. The result (2007, Eq. 4 for n = 3) is exact for the interpolant, has no quadrature error, and is a vectorised sum over intervals for every output point at once (kk.pv_over_s2, via 1/(s^2 - \omega^2) = [1/(s - \omega) - 1/(s + \omega)]/(2\omega)). Two thousand points transform in a fraction of a second; the same transform by adaptive quadrature took OptiCFRC minutes.

As implemented. At the two endpoints s_0 = a and s_N = b there is no partner interval, and the principal value genuinely diverges like \mp P(c)\ln|s - c| as c \to a, b. That divergence is kept (with the logarithm clamped at 10^{-12}), because in the derivative-form phase it cancels analytically against the boundary term above, which uses the same clamped logarithm. Dropping the endpoint logarithm, an easy mistake, leaves a spurious logarithmic spike at both edges of the corrected phase. The closed form was validated against OptiCFRC’s quadrature on the same cubic spline to 5\cdot10^{-3} in phase, and against the exact phase of Lorentz models.

Multiply subtractive relations

If the true value of the function is known at anchor frequencies \omega_1, \dots, \omega_n inside [a, b], the error there is known, \Delta_i = \chi'(\omega_i) - \chi'^{ab}_{KK}(\omega_i), and the multiply subtractive Kramers–Kronig relation (2007, Eq. 6, 8, 9; Palmer, Williams & Budde 1998) corrects the truncated transform by the Lagrange polynomial in \omega^2 through those errors:

\chi'^{ab}_{MSKK}(\omega) = \chi'^{ab}_{KK}(\omega) + \sum_{i=1}^n \Delta_i \prod_{j \ne i} \frac{\omega^2 - \omega_j^2}{\omega_i^2 - \omega_j^2}

(kk.mskk; the same for \theta). It is exact at the anchors. With one anchor it is a constant shift; with several, a polynomial that oscillates away from the anchors, which the paper shows to give poor estimates between distant anchors (2007, Fig. 8): the polynomial has no physical reason to follow the error.

Constrained finite-range correction

Model the error instead of interpolating it. Choose a correction f^{ab}(\omega; \mathbf x) (for \chi') or g^{ab}(\omega; \mathbf x) (for \theta) with m parameters and find \mathbf x by least squares on the n \ge m anchors (2007, Eq. 12, 16):

F(\mathbf x) = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[f^{ab}(\omega_i; \mathbf x) - \Delta_i\right]^2 \to \min, \qquad \chi'^{ab}_{CFRC}(\omega) = \chi'^{ab}_{KK}(\omega) + f^{ab}(\omega; \mathbf x) .

The correction models come from the physics of the missing ranges:

Extension of \chi'' (2007, Eq. 14; kk.ChiExtension). Continue \chi'' below a and above b by piecewise polynomials \xi(s; \mathbf x_l) and \zeta(s; \mathbf x_h) through a few knots, continuous with the data at a and b, zero at s = 0 and beyond the last knot, and take their transform as the correction:

f^{ab}(\omega; \mathbf x) = \frac{2}{\pi}\,P\!\!\int_0^a \frac{s\,\xi(s)}{s^2 - \omega^2}\,ds + \frac{2}{\pi}\,P\!\!\int_b^\infty \frac{s\,\zeta(s)}{s^2 - \omega^2}\,ds .

The free knot values are the parameters, bounded to be non-negative (passivity of the extension). As implemented: piecewise-linear extensions through the knots (the paper uses cubic splines; smoothness is a lower-priority refinement), evaluated by the same closed-form kernel.

Weighted poles for the phase (2007, Eq. 18; kk.PhaseLogTerms). The missing parts of d\ln r/ds are replaced by Dirac deltas of weight x_1 at x_2 < a and x_3 at x_4 > b, whose transform is explicit:

g^{ab}(\omega; \mathbf x) = x_1 \ln\left|\frac{\omega + x_2}{\omega - x_2}\right| + x_3 \ln\left|\frac{\omega + x_4}{\omega - x_4}\right| .

Each term is the phase contribution of a single sharp feature outside the range. Its variant with a linear term, x_1\ln|(\omega + x_2)/(\omega - x_2)| + x_3\,\omega (kk.PhaseLogLinear), is the model of the paper’s \mathrm{NdGaO_{3}} case (Table II).

The positiveness constraint (2007, Table II, quartz CFRC-2 and \mathrm{NdGaO_{3}}). For a semitransparent crystal the phase nearly vanishes between the reststrahlen bands, so \theta(\omega) \ge 0 on [a, b] carries real information, enough on its own to fix the two-pole model with no anchor. As implemented (kk.cfrc(positive=True)): the constraint is a hinge penalty w_+\min(\theta_{corr}, 0) added to the residuals, weight 10 by default. With the constraint alone the problem is one-sided (any correction that keeps \theta \ge 0 satisfies it), so, when no anchor is given, a weak pull of the corrected function toward zero, \lambda\,\theta_{corr}/(\max|\theta_{KK}|\sqrt N) with \lambda = 1, is added: the corrected phase comes down until its minima touch zero, which is what the paper’s Fig. 8 shows. On a quartz-like model truncated at 400 \mathrm{cm}^{-1} the mean phase error falls from 0.20 to 0.009 with no anchor. The pull is not applied when anchors are given.

The parameters are found by bounded least squares (scipy.optimize.least_squares) from a starting guess (poles just outside the range, small strengths, zero knot values), with the poles bounded to [0.2, 5] times their start.

Choosing anchors and models

From the paper’s examples (Table I and II) and the tests: anchors near the borders of the range and well separated determine the corrections best; one anchor and a two-knot extension already give a nearly perfect \chi' for a glass on 250–2500 \mathrm{cm}^{-1}; the two-pole phase model with two or four anchors, or with the positiveness constraint, gives the phase of quartz to better than 10^{-2}; MSKK with the same anchors is good only near them. Whenever a model fit of the same spectrum exists, its phase is the natural check of a Kramers–Kronig result, and the session graph of Part I makes the comparison one node.

Numerical causality check

For a term or formula \chi(\omega), kk.causality_error computes \chi' from \chi'' on a uniform grid [0, \omega_{max}] (default 40 000 \mathrm{cm}^{-1}, step 1) by the Maclaurin rule, which evaluates the principal value at even grid points from odd ones (no singular point is ever sampled), and returns \max|\chi'_{KK} - \chi'| / \max|\chi'| at a few points. Every built-in term passes below 10^{-2}; the residual is the weight of the term beyond \omega_{max} and the discretisation, not a failure of causality.
