The test ledger

Every check difes has passed through, in the order it was run, each one graded on what it proved and against what. The presentation card of the app: the good results are credible because the open ones are listed next to them.

A fit program gives three things: the curve it draws for a model, the parameters it stops at, and the way a person reaches them. The question that matters to someone who has used FOCUS every day is not whether difes is modern but whether it is right. This ledger answers it test by test. Each record says what was asked, what difes was compared against and how strong that reference is, what data were used and where they come from, what was done, what came out, and whether the objective was met, on a five-level scale. One source holds the records (data/tests.yml); this page, the card pages and the PDF of the whole ledger are generated from it, so every future test is one more entry.

How to read the ledger

Each test has a card below and a page of its own. The card shows the identifier, the grade, the class of reference, the question the test asks and the one-line verdict. The page holds the full record:

  • Objective. One question that can be answered yes or no.
  • Reference. What difes is compared against: FOCUS’s exported numbers, FOCUS’s drawn curves, a printed table, or physics itself.
  • Data. Source, provider, how obtained (an export from FOCUS, the vector paths of a PDF, a digitised bitmap), files, number of points, range, and the precision of the data themselves.
  • Physics covered. The profiles, the sample geometry, the quantity, the special constructions.
  • Procedure. Forward evaluation, refit from a perturbed start, fit to data, inversion, round trip.
  • Result. The numbers.
  • Verdict, and the open point if there is one.
  • Where to look. The script, the automated test, the page of the site that discusses it, the figure.

The identifier, the date, the difes version and the log entry give the evolution: the ledger is in the order the tests were run, from the physics identities of the first release to the FOCUS expert’s own fits.

The scales

Two scales per test, one matrix for the whole app.

Grade of achievement
A · exact agreement at the precision of the reference itself (the file's digits, machine precision), nothing fitted
B · within the data curves agree within the data's own scatter or the digitisation precision; parameters agree where the problem is well posed
C · explained a difference exists, its origin is identified and quantified, and difes's side is the defensible one
D · open a difference is observed and a hypothesis exists; an author or a file is needed to confirm it
E · blocked the test cannot be run yet, and the reason is stated
Class of reference
class I FOCUS's numerical output: curves exported from FOCUS to seven significant digits, or FOCUS's own formulas as written in its files
class II FOCUS's output as a vector figure: fitted curves and data read exactly from the paths of a PDF
class III printed tables and bitmap figures: transcribed parameters and figures digitised to about \(5\times10^{-3}\) of the axis
class IV physics alone: identities, limits, closed forms and independent programs, with no FOCUS reference

The class says how much a grade is worth: an A against class I is the strongest statement the ledger can make, and the ledger has several. A D is not a failure of difes; in every D so far the difference is between a publication’s table and its own figure, and the question is put to the author. An E is a test that is waiting for an input, listed so that the gaps are as visible as the results.

The coverage matrix at the end of the page crosses the capabilities of difes, profile by profile and geometry by geometry, with the best grade any test reached on each. Blank cells are the roadmap.

Where the evidence stands

Read from the top down, the ledger says this. Everything difes computes was first checked against physics, with no reference program in sight (T01 to T05). Then, wherever FOCUS wrote its own numbers down, difes met them to the digits of the file: Irati’s final anisotropic model, FOCUS’s quartz, phosphate glass and Raman sample projects, FOCUS’s own libraries and example models, four one-band exports (T06, T11, T15 to T20). Where FOCUS’s result exists only as a drawn curve, difes lands on it to the width of the line, in the fluorides and sapphire of FOCUS’s authors and in the FOCUS expert’s own thesis (T12, T13, T27). The two places where difes and FOCUS differ are understood and quantified: FOCUS’s approximation of the Voigt function, a part in a thousand of a band, and a shallow eight-parameter fit where the two programs stopped in different minima (T18, T20, T28). What remains open is between publications and their own tables, not between the programs (T22, T24 to T26), and what is blocked waits for a file (T29 to T31). A second reference program entered the ledger on 4 October: RefFIT’s variational dielectric function and its line shapes are in difes and tested against physics (T32, T33); RefFIT’s own output arrived on 5 and 6 October, Irati’s run of the protocol, two fits of her own and the TUTORIAL folder of a RefFIT installation, and met difes to the six digits RefFIT exports; on RefFIT’s own data the two fitters stop at the same minima (T34, T42, T43, T46, T47). The strongest test remains T31, and it has not been run yet: one of Iñigo González de Arrieta’s everyday projects, opened in difes in front of him.

    What this is

    The test ledger of difes, a program for dielectric-function models, reflectivity, emissivity, Kramers–Kronig and fitting of infrared spectra, built as a personal project to give the group an open and documented counterpart of FOCUS. Every check the program has passed through is listed here with its grade, its data and its result, next to the validation pages the checks come from and the complete manual: how the app looks and works, and the physics it implements, equation by equation.
